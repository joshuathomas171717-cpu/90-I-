## Three things this gameweek is being used to test

Named now, before the results, so that they cannot be chosen afterwards to fit whatever happened.

**1. Draw avoidance.** Zero draw calls in ten fixtures. That is not automatically wrong — a draw is
rarely the single most likely outcome, which is why a well-calibrated model can go a long time without
picking one — but the underlying rate matters more than the count. Over the season the question is
whether a stated 25% draw lands about a quarter of the time. Nine more draws than the model expects
across the campaign would say the draw probability is systematically too low, and that is the single
most likely place for this model to be wrong.

**2. The scoreline column.** Eight of the ten projections carry a 1-1 scoreline. That is the mode of a
Poisson distribution with these goal expectations, so it is arithmetically correct — and it is also
nearly useless as a second signal, because 1-1 is a plausible score in almost every football match.
Watching whether the projected scoreline ever matches the actual result is how we decide whether the
column is information or decoration. If it turns out to be decoration, the receipts page should say so
rather than keep printing it as though it meant something.

**3. The toss-up band.** Half this gameweek is a coin-flip by the model's own reckoning. A toss-up
that goes the wrong way is not evidence of anything; ten of them going the wrong way would be. The
specific measurement is the 35–45% band: if calls made at ~38% land materially below 38% across the
season, the model is overconfident in exactly the matches it is least sure about, which is the worst
possible place to be wrong.

## Carried forward from the 2025–26 replay

The replay finished at 176/380 (46.3%) with a mean RPS of 0.2184, ahead of every baseline on the same
fixtures. Two things are still open from it and need live matches, not more backtesting:

- **Calibration at the top.** The replay's calibration table was checked band by band; the 65%+ band
  has the fewest observations and the largest error bars.
- **Promoted-club factors.** The regression factors for promoted sides are the model's hardest-coded
  assumption (they were fitted on previous seasons, not on this one). Coventry, Hull and Ipswich are
  all in this season's promoted group, and their first ten matches are the only real test available.
